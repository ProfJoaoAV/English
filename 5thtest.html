next to / beside — next to the café

inside / outside / on — inside the restaurant | on the terrace
along (movement along a line) — walking along the street

into / out of (movement in/out) — going into the shop
past / through / near — cycling past the library | near the forest
Place
"The bakery is between the café and the market."
Movement
"She walked along the river path."
📈 Comparative of superiority Grammar
Short adjectives: adjective + -er than
small → smaller than | big → bigger than | quiet → quieter than
Doubling rule: CVC → double final consonant: big→bigger, hot→hotter
Long adjectives (2+ syllables): more + adjective + than
comfortable → more comfortable than | beautiful → more beautiful than
Irregular: good → better than | bad → worse than | far → farther/further than
Short
"A village is quieter than a city."
Long
"A train is more comfortable than a bus."
Irregular
"My score is better than yours."
🏆 Superlative of superiority Grammar
Short adjectives: the + adjective + -est
small → the smallest | big → the biggest | tall → the tallest
Long adjectives: the most + adjective
expensive → the most expensive | spectacular → the most spectacular
Irregular: good → the best | bad → the worst | far → the farthest
Short
"This is the tallest tree in the forest."
Long
"This is the most beautiful village."
🕐 Past simple: to be & there to be Grammar
To be:
I / he / she / it → was | you / we / they → were
Negative: wasn't / weren't | Question: Was it…? / Were they…?
There to be:
Singular → There was (a lake, a library)
Plural → There were (skyscrapers, parks)
Negative: There wasn't / There weren't
Was
"The village was peaceful."
There was
"There was a library."
There were
"There were lots of trees."

Their house was a wooden chalet with five bedrooms and a large terrace where the whole family could enjoy breakfast together. In Pinehaven, on Saturday mornings, the villagers used to visit the weekly craft fair and buy handmade pottery, candles, and woollen scarves.
